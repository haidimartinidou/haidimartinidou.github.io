I'm Haidi Martinidou, a neuroscientist based in Thessaloniki. I hold an MSc in Psychology and Neuroscience of Mental Health (Merit) from King's College London and a BSc (Hons) in Neuroscience from the University of Glasgow. Since July 2026 I have been an affiliate researcher at Glasgow with Dr Ourania Varsou.

The question I want to work on is how chronic stress changes the brain's reward and memory systems to produce anhedonia and apathy, and why some brains withstand the same stress. Longer term, I want to ask whether protecting these systems earlier can delay or prevent dementia.

I have come at it from several scales. My BSc thesis used UK Biobank data from over 500,000 adults to test whether a Mediterranean-style diet is associated with lower odds of depression. Dr Donald Lyall and I are now preparing it as a preprint. My MSc dissertation was a narrative synthesis of fMRI, EEG, PET, TMS and tDCS studies on fairness processing and reward-risk prediction. Valuation and prediction-error signals appeared across methods, but the literature could not show whether an unfair outcome drives learning or reflects a stable way of weighting outcomes. At IQVIA I recruited patients for non-interventional studies across more than twenty therapeutic areas. At Glasgow I now co-lead, as joint first author, a mixed-methods evaluation of a medical exchange programme for students from conflict-affected settings. With Dr Paschalis Theotokis at Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, I am first author on a conceptual review of whether sustained AI use reshapes brain networks.

I am applying to neuroscience PhD programmes for 2027, focusing on stress, motivation and memory. I want to lead a lab, teach, and eventually carry findings into everyday prevention.
