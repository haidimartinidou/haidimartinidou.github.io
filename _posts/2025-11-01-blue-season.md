Flu season is old news; now all my friends are having a bad day, week, or even month. According to them, of course. It's a blue season.

Not to undermine every individual drama; every single one of them is horrid. From missing buses to flights, and from a bad hair day to an emergency room visit. Ranking or comparing is no use: My friends are going through a mental health flu season.

It's November. Such an indifferent month to live your tragedy. Yet again, this month is characterised by Q4 evaluations, universities are pushing, and we realise how much we spent during the summer.

All in all, madness!

Or actually: Just biology.

Never will I stop vouching for understanding our brain as the organ that it is. We aren't air and vibes. We are organisms that are the sum of many parts. One of which is our brain: There to perceive the world for us and tell us when we are sad.

In autumn days become shorter, and we spend more time in the darkness. This means our circadian rhythm is all messed up, and thus we are experiencing a mini jet-lag, without even the thrill of a trip to another continent. Just your old routine, but with a constant brain fog. Cognitive skills, along with emotional regulation, are out of whack, and getting things done at work feels daunting.

Acknowledging the absence of environmental reward signals (i.e., the sun or the warmer weather) is a hack that normalises this lack of energy, making it feel less concerning.

But again, less sunlight translates to less environmental reward. This is combined with an eagerness to stay in. Socialising doesn't seem so breezy anymore, and crawling under a blanket on the couch watching old Disney is just so easy. Like a bear about to hibernate. Everything is slower and we are filled with hormones that make stress itself more stressful. We are a tad sadder and heavier.

In days when things feel heavier, we can acknowledge that our brain needs some help and unapologetically take a minute to be human.
