Delusion is one of the keys to happiness.

Lesson one: Spilling coffee is good luck.

Some Southern Europeans would relate. Some would even see my spilt coffee and raise me some bird faeces. The intellectuals will note all the money that comes after hand itching, eye hurting and all that jazz. (Statistically significant correlations are available in the literature; you should check it out.)

And I can't help but wonder: Who's grandma came up with that? I'd love to know her insights on mood disorders and the key to happiness.

Imagine a life where all sorts of misfortunes were viewed as moments of luck. Because what is life, other than our own perception of it?

I have a spoon tattoo on my forearm. One of its many meanings has to do with our perception of reality and cognitive biases. Depression is oftentimes characterised/caused by phenomena such as Catastrophizing. This is a distorted thinking pattern in which you think that the worst possible outcomes are the most likely ones, or that your tiny mistakes will bring the world to its end. Ergo, perceiving life through catastrophe-tinted glasses brings you closer to depression. Because it's all about perception.

You have two choices when a bird leaves their "trace" on your outdoor table at lunch:

A) Laugh and say it's "good luck", because your family was healthily delusional

B) Think this is simply because you are a magnet for misfortunes, and let your day be ruined

Perception of life is not as easy, though. Our brain is the organ that processes data from the real world, and if this brain is hacked and reprogrammed to find the bad in the good, the glass will always look half-empty. Some brains are not able to change their algorithm on demand. But it's worth trying to train our mind to get rid of negative thinking, and become more...delusional.

And maybe seniors in the Mediterranean are truly the guidance we might need on gloomy afternoons. Our brains are designed to assign meanings. And repetition of these associations, as arbitrary as they may sound, might bring enough delusion to help us overcome our toxic suppressions.

After all, you spilt your 12$ coffee on your white linen; Could you be any luckier today?
